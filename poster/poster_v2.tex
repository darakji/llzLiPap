\begin{tcolorbox}[posterbox,title=Motivation: the interface decides the battery]
\justifying
Solid-state batteries live or die at the Li-metal\,$|$\,electrolyte interface.
Resolving that chemistry demands molecular dynamics on \textbf{hundreds to
thousands of atoms} --- precisely the regime machine-learned interatomic
potentials (MLIPs) were built for.
\vspace{0.4em}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \LLZO\ against Li metal is \textbf{the} prototypical case: a metal and
        a complex oxide, with strong chemical and structural mismatch.
  \item One interface supercell is \textbf{420--1378 atoms}. Orientation,
        termination and Li-site disorder open a configuration space that no
        direct-DFT campaign can sample.
  \item Foundation MLIPs are trained overwhelmingly on near-equilibrium
        \emph{bulk}, so interfaces are out of distribution and forces are
        \bad{systematically underestimated} where the physics lives.
\end{itemize}
\vspace{0.3em}
\begin{center}\Large
\key{Two problems: what reference method can label these cells at all, and
which cells are worth labelling.}
\end{center}
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{0.7em}

\begin{tcolorbox}[posterbox,title=\DFTFE: the engine that makes this possible]
\justifying
\textbf{Every label in this work is a \DFTFE\ calculation, and the campaign
could not be run on any other reference method.} \DFTFE\ solves Kohn--Sham DFT
in \textbf{real space} on a systematically improvable \textbf{finite-element}
basis --- massively parallel, GPU-accelerated, and the code behind the
\key{2023 ACM Gordon Bell Prize}.
\vspace{0.5em}

first liquid-like metal layer occupy a \textbf{band of finite thickness}, and

\vspace{0.5em}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \key{Semi-periodic boundary conditions.} A finite-element basis is
        local, so periodicity is a \emph{choice per direction}:
        \texttt{pbc=[True,True,False]}. A plane-wave basis cannot do this ---
        3D periodicity forces a \bad{sandwich} with a spurious second
        interface and an image dipole.
  \item \key{Scale.} Real-space domain decomposition puts every label at
        \good{420--1378 atoms}, so the potential is \good{trained at the size
        it is deployed} --- no extrapolation up from small periodic
        approximants.
\end{itemize}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\small\textbf{Setup:} GGA--PBE $\bullet$ SPMS pseudopotentials $\bullet$ FE
order 7 $\bullet$ mesh 1.2\,\AA\ $\bullet$ refinement radius 8\,\AA\ $\bullet$
smearing 500\,K $\bullet$ SCF $5\times10^{-5}$\,Ha $\bullet$ zero Dirichlet in
$z$ $\bullet$ OLCF Frontier, 64 nodes $\times$ 4 MI250X.}
\end{tcolorbox}

\figcap{Each round samples with the current model, keeps the physical frames,
embeds them in the model's own latent space, selects by farthest-point
sampling seeded on everything already labelled, labels with \DFTFE, and
re-fine-tunes the foundation model on the cumulative set.}
\vspace{0.5em}

Candidates are embedded with the \textbf{current} model: the rotationally
invariant ($\ell=0$) scalars of the final interaction block give
$\mathbf{z}_i\in\mathbb{R}^{256}$ per atom, mean-pooled to
$\mathbf{Z}_S=\frac{1}{N_S}\sum_i\mathbf{z}_i$. Distance here is
\textbf{the model's own measure of chemical difference}.
\vspace{0.3em}
\begin{equation*}
k^{*}=\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{k\in\mathcal{P}}\,
\min_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\lVert \mathbf{Z}_k-\mathbf{Z}_s\rVert_2,
\qquad
\delta_k=\min_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\lVert \mathbf{Z}_k-\mathbf{Z}_s\rVert_2,
\qquad \rho=\max_k \delta_k
\end{equation*}
Seeding on the set \emph{already labelled} means a near-duplicate
\good{can never be selected} --- de-duplication is guaranteed by
construction, not enforced by a tuned threshold. A round closes when the
worst-covered candidate falls inside the chosen coverage percentile of
$C(d)=|\{k:\delta_k\le d\}|/|\mathcal{P}|$, so \textbf{there is no guesswork
about when to stop}.
\end{tcolorbox}

\begin{tcolorbox}[posterbox,title=Conclusions,colframe=inkblue]
\justifying
\begin{itemize}
  \item \key{\DFTFE\ is the enabler.} Real-space finite elements give
        semi-periodic boundary conditions --- impossible in a plane-wave basis
        --- and ground states on 420--1378-atom cells, resolving the interface
        as a finite region bounded by two free surfaces.
  \item \key{Coverage-driven latent selection} spends a fixed \DFTFE\ budget
        on the configurations farthest from everything already labelled, and
        provably never re-labels a near-duplicate.
  \item \key{LoRA} ($r=5$, $<$1\% of parameters) absorbs all 108 labels with
        the pretrained bulk chemistry left intact.
  \item \key{Force error cut 40\%} against the foundation model, improving
        every round, with energy at chemical accuracy throughout.
  \item \key{The acquisition rule is independently validated} ($r=+0.85$):
        the method is measured, not asserted.
\end{itemize}
\vspace{0.3em}
Architecture-agnostic: latent FPS, open-boundary real-space labels and LoRA
transfer directly to Li\,$|$\,LPS, Li\,$|$\,LiPON, NaSICON and other
solid-state interfaces.
\end{tcolorbox}
